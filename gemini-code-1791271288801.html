Past Simple, Past Continuous & Past Perfect

1. Past Simple

When to use:

  • To describe the main events of a story.
  • For completed actions or events in the past.
  • To indicate a specific point in time when the action occurred.

Form: V2 (regular verbs: +ed, irregular verbs: 2nd column).
Negative: didn't + V1.

Example: Giorgio Armani studied medicine in his 20s. Later, in the 1960s, he became a fashion designer.

2. Past Continuous

When to use:

  • To give background information about events that happened at the same time as one or more main events.
  • To indicate an action that was in progress at a specific time in the past.

Form: was / were + Ving.
Negative: wasn't / weren't + Ving.

Example: Marie Curie received her second Nobel Prize in 1911, while she was working at Sorbonne University.

Time Markers: WHEN and WHILE

We use when or while to introduce a background activity, or to show that two background activities happened at the same time.

  • While + long action (Past Continuous).
    While I was watching TV, Jim knocked on the door.
  • When + short action (Past Simple). We use when (not while) to introduce the main event.
    When Jim knocked on the door, I was watching TV.

3. Past Perfect

When to use:

We use the past perfect to give information about background events that happened before the main events.

Form: had + V3 (past participle).
Negative: hadn't + V3.

He had written 600 pieces of music...
Past Simple
...before Mozart died in 1791.

Example: When we met (Past Simple), I 'd been (Past Perfect) in the US for six months.

Part 1: Choose the correct option

1. I was very late. When I arrived, the conference _____.
Incorrect. The conference started before you arrived. We use Past Perfect for an action completed before another action in the past. Correct answer: C. had already started.
2. When he heard the police knocking on the door, he ____ under the bed.
Incorrect. He hid after he heard the knock. These are consecutive actions in a story. We use Past Simple for consecutive actions. Correct answer: A. hid.
3. The police arrested him because he _____ a gun.
Incorrect. He was in the process of carrying a gun at the moment they arrested him. This is background information for a specific time in the past. Correct answer: B. was carrying.
4. When they arrived at the police station, he said that he _____ anything wrong.
Incorrect. He didn't do anything wrong before they arrived at the station. We use Past Perfect for actions that happened before another past action. Correct answer: C. hadn't done.
5. When he said that he loved me, I knew that he _____.
Incorrect. He was in the process of telling a lie exactly at the moment he said "I love you". We use Past Continuous for an action in progress. Correct answer: B. was lying.
6. I looked at him and I knew he _____ in a fight, because he had a black eye.
Incorrect. The fight happened before you looked at him. We use Past Perfect for an action that happened before another past event. Correct answer: C. had been.
7. He pulled out a gun and everybody _____ away.
Incorrect. First he pulled out a gun, then everybody ran away. These are consecutive events in a sequence. We use Past Simple. Correct answer: A. ran.
8. When I was a child, we _____ anywhere at weekends; it was boring.
Incorrect. This describes a repeated habit or state in the past. We use Past Simple for past habits. Correct answer: A. never went.
9. When he retired he _____ in the company for more than 50 years.
Incorrect. He worked there for 50 years up to the point when he retired. We use Past Perfect to talk about a duration leading up to a point in the past. Correct answer: C. had been.
10. I _____ to the road and we crashed.
Incorrect. Not paying attention was an ongoing action (background event) when the sudden short action (crashed) interrupted it. Correct answer: B. wasn't paying attention.
